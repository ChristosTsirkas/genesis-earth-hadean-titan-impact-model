# AI Assistance Disclosure

This research paper and its accompanying computational repository were developed
as an independent interdisciplinary project with substantial assistance from
generative artificial-intelligence systems. The author’s professional background
is in software development rather than in astrobiology, planetary science,
geochemistry, celestial mechanics, or impact hydrodynamics. This is stated
explicitly because the project addresses a technically demanding problem across
several scientific disciplines and does not claim formal specialist qualification
in each of them.

The central hypothesis, overall physical scenario, research questions, modeling
objectives, interpretation pursued, and decision to develop and publish the
project belong to the author. AI systems were used extensively as research and
development tools: to map relevant literature; explain and connect concepts from
different disciplines; assist with mathematical formulation and dimensional
checking; develop, debug, and revise Python code; organize parameter studies and
calibration runs; examine numerical outputs for internal consistency; identify
weaknesses and counterarguments; and assist with technical writing and revision.

AI-generated material was not accepted automatically. Proposed equations,
assumptions, citations, parameter values, computational procedures,
interpretations, and textual revisions were selected, modified, rejected, or
retained through an iterative process directed by the author. This review is not
presented as independent validation by specialists. The calculations and
reduced-order simulations constitute a transparent and testable exploratory
framework, not a substitute for peer review, laboratory evidence, expert
replication, or high-resolution hydrodynamic modeling.

AI systems are not presented as authors, scientific authorities, or sources of
empirical evidence. Responsibility for presenting the hypothesis, organizing
the argument, disclosing its assumptions and limitations, and approving the
final contents remains with the named human author. The frozen configurations,
computational procedures, calibration history, generated outputs, and principal
limitations are distributed to facilitate external assessment and replication.
